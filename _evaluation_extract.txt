1. Executive Summary 
This research investigates plant biodiversity and environmental conditions within Tunas Muda 
School's grounds, aiming to identify opportunities to improve ecosystem health and encourage 
environmental responsibility among students. 
Using photographic observations of trees, ornamental plants, landscaped areas and 
surrounding infrastructure, our group examined the visible diversity of plant life and its 
relationship with the school environment. 
Our initial findings indicate that the school supports several types of vegetation, including 
flowering trees, palms, broad-leaved tropical plants and ornamental shrubs. However, some 
plants exhibit visible signs of leaf discoloration and damage, while several planting areas are 
surrounded by paved surfaces and infrastructure. 
Although these observations do not necessarily indicate poor ecosystem health, they highlight 
opportunities for further investigation, improved plant maintenance, biodiversity conservation 
and environmental education. 
We propose developing a student-led biodiversity awareness website, introducing plant 
identification labels, conducting regular environmental monitoring and encouraging the school 
community to participate in conservation activities. 
Through this project, we hope to demonstrate that environmental sustainability begins with 
understanding and appreciating the ecosystems within our immediate surroundings.

2. Introduction and Background 
2.1 Background 
Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms within an environment, including plants, 
animals, fungi and microorganisms. Plant biodiversity is particularly important because plants 
serve as primary producers, provide habitats, support food webs and contribute to 
environmental stability. 
Within a school environment, plants also provide shade, improve the appearance of outdoor 
spaces and create opportunities for students to learn about ecological relationships. 
At Tunas Muda School, we noticed that the surrounding vegetation is sometimes overlooked in 
everyday school activities. This observation inspired our group to investigate the school's plant 
biodiversity and explore how students could become more environmentally responsible. 
Rather than simply identifying plants, our project aims to understand their ecological 
significance, evaluate observable environmental conditions and recommend practical 
improvements. 
2.2 Research Question 
How does plant biodiversity contribute to the environmental health of Tunas Muda 
School, and what practical actions can the school community take to preserve and 
improve its ecosystem? 
2.3 Research Objectives 
1.​ Identify and document different types of plants found within the school grounds. 
2.​ Observe visible indicators of plant health and environmental conditions. 
3.​ Analyze the ecological importance of the observed vegetation. 
4.​ Identify potential environmental challenges and areas for improvement. 
5.​ Develop practical recommendations to increase environmental awareness and 
sustainability. 
6.​ Communicate our findings through a research website and a proposal to the school's 
Vice Principal, Ms. Gia.

3.3 Research Limitations 
The research is based on four photographs rather than a comprehensive ecological survey. 
Consequently, the findings cannot establish the total number of plant species, population sizes, 
soil quality, wildlife diversity or long-term ecosystem health. 
Plant identification is also preliminary, as accurate species identification may require closer 
photographs of leaves, flowers, fruits and stems. 
No soil tests, biodiversity indices, interviews or repeated monitoring results have been provided. 
These would strengthen the research if conducted in future sessions. 
4. Research Findings and Observations 
4.1 Observation 1 — Flowering Tree Near Recreational Facilities 
Photographic Evidence: Image 1 
The first photograph shows a mature, branching tree with numerous rounded, bilobed leaves 
and scattered pink-purple flowers. These characteristics suggest that the tree belongs to the 
genus Bauhinia, commonly known as the orchid tree. 

===== PAGE 4 =====
Visible characteristics: 
●​ A relatively large canopy with multiple branches. 
●​ Green, rounded leaves with a characteristic divided shape. 
●​ Scattered pink-purple flowers. 
●​ Some yellowing leaves among predominantly green foliage. 
●​ Surrounding recreational infrastructure, including seating areas and paved surfaces. 
Ecological significance: 
Flowering trees can provide nectar and other resources for visiting insects, while their branches 
and foliage may offer shelter for small organisms. 
Their canopies also provide shade and can reduce the amount of direct sunlight reaching the 
ground. 
Environmental assessment: 
The tree appears established and retains substantial foliage. However, some leaves are yellow, 
and the photograph does not provide sufficient evidence to determine whether this reflects 
normal leaf aging, seasonal changes or environmental stress. 
Recommended action: Monitor leaf condition over time, document flowering periods and 
investigate whether pollinating insects visit the flowers. 
4.2 Observation 2 — Ornamental Planting Beds 
Photographic Evidence: Image 2 
The second photograph presents several cultivated tropical plants growing in rectangular white 
planters and a larger planting bed. 
The visible plants include variegated ornamental foliage, deeply lobed leaves and large 
arrow-shaped leaves. 
Preliminary plant identification: 
●​ Aglaonema species or cultivars — likely represented by the variegated plants with green, 
cream and pinkish leaf patterns. 
●​ Alocasia or a related aroid genus — possibly represented by the large, upright, 
arrow-shaped leaves. 
●​ Thaumatophyllum or a related genus — potentially represented by the deeply lobed 
foliage. 
Visible characteristics: 

===== PAGE 5 =====
●​ Several different leaf shapes, sizes and color patterns. 
●​ Organized planting containers. 
●​ Exposed soil surrounding plant bases. 
●​ Some yellowing leaves, brown leaf edges and localized damage. 
●​ Plants growing close together in a landscaped environment. 
Ecological significance: 
These ornamental plants increase the visible variety of vegetation within the school. Their 
foliage provides additional plant cover and contributes to the complexity of the landscaped 
environment. 
However, the presence of different leaf colors and shapes does not automatically indicate high 
species diversity, as some plants may be cultivars of the same species. 
Environmental assessment: 
The planting beds contain mostly green foliage, suggesting that many plants remain actively 
growing. Some individual leaves show discoloration or damage, but their causes cannot be 
determined from photographs alone. 
Recommended action: Inspect irrigation and drainage, remove fully dead foliage when 
appropriate, and monitor whether leaf damage increases over time. 
4.3 Observation 3 — Landscaped School Pathway 
Photographic Evidence: Image 3 
The third photograph shows a landscaped school environment containing a flowering tree, 
trimmed shrubs, a pedestrian pathway, outdoor seating and recreational infrastructure. 
The flowering tree appears similar to the one documented in Image 1 and may represent the 
same individual or species. 
Visible characteristics: 
●​ A flowering tree with an extensive canopy. 
●​ Multiple maintained shrubs or small trees. 
●​ Exposed soil surrounding some planted areas. 
●​ Paved walkways and seating facilities. 
●​ Vegetation distributed alongside human activity spaces. 
Ecological significance: 

===== PAGE 6 =====
The combination of trees and shrubs provides multiple vegetation layers. Different plant heights 
and structures can create a wider range of potential microhabitats than a landscape containing 
only grass. 
Trees also offer shade for students and help make outdoor spaces more comfortable. 
Environmental assessment: 
The vegetation is visibly maintained and integrated into the school's outdoor infrastructure. 
However, the photograph alone cannot establish whether the landscaping supports substantial 
wildlife diversity or whether the existing species are predominantly native or introduced. 
Recommended action: Establish a biodiversity observation zone where students can record 
plant species, flowering activity and visible wildlife without disturbing the habitat. 
4.4 Observation 4 — Tropical Palms Near a Wall 
Photographic Evidence: Image 4 
The fourth photograph shows several palms growing alongside a boundary wall, with additional 
vegetation nearby. 
The palms have elongated, feather-like leaves consisting of multiple narrow leaflets. 
Preliminary plant identification: 
These plants belong to the palm family, Arecaceae. They resemble commonly cultivated 
ornamental palms, although the exact species cannot be confirmed from the photograph. 
Visible characteristics: 
●​ Tall, slender stems. 
●​ Long, arching, feather-like leaves. 
●​ Dense foliage at different heights. 
●​ Vegetation growing near a wall. 
●​ Limited visible space between some plants. 
Ecological significance: 
Palms contribute to vegetation structure and may provide shelter or resources for small 
organisms, depending on their species and local ecological interactions. 
Their leaves can also contribute organic matter to the soil as they naturally age and decompose. 
Environmental assessment: 

===== PAGE 7 =====
The palms appear predominantly green and upright. The photograph does not reveal sufficient 
information about soil conditions, root development or long-term plant health. 
Recommended action: Monitor plant growth, inspect the surrounding soil and ensure 
maintenance practices are appropriate for the identified palm species. 
5. Overall Biodiversity Analysis 
5.1 Diversity of Plant Forms 
The photographic evidence demonstrates that Tunas Muda School contains multiple forms of 
vegetation, including: 
●​ Mature flowering trees. 
●​ Ornamental shrubs and small trees. 
●​ Broad-leaved tropical plants. 
●​ Variegated foliage plants. 
●​ Palms and other landscaped vegetation. 
This suggests that the school possesses a visually varied planted environment. 
However, because the photographs cover only selected locations, they cannot be used to 
calculate total species richness or establish the overall biodiversity of the school. 
5.2 Plant Health and Environmental Conditions 
Most photographed plants retain substantial green foliage, indicating the presence of living, 
established vegetation. 
Nevertheless, several ornamental plants show visible discoloration or leaf damage. 
Possible explanations include natural aging, water imbalance, environmental exposure, nutrient 
availability or pests. Further examination would be required before identifying any particular 
cause. 
The presence of paved surfaces near planting areas also highlights the importance of 
investigating soil permeability, root space and water drainage. 
5.3 Human Influence on the Ecosystem 
The photographs show that vegetation is closely integrated with school facilities, including 
pathways, seating areas, recreational spaces and walls. 
This relationship creates opportunities for environmental education because students encounter 
plants throughout their daily routines. 

===== PAGE 8 =====
However, landscaped ecosystems also require management. Plant selection, watering 
practices, pruning and waste management can influence their long-term ecological value. 
The available evidence does not establish that students have damaged or neglected the plants. 
Student awareness and behavior should therefore be investigated separately rather than 
assumed.

7. Proposed Environmental Improvements 
Recommendation 1 — Create a School Biodiversity Website 
Develop an accessible website documenting the plants found within Tunas Muda School. 
The website should include verified plant names, photographs, ecological roles, observation 
locations and practical conservation information. 
This would transform the group's findings into an educational resource available to students and 
teachers. 
Recommendation 2 — Introduce Plant Identification Labels 

===== PAGE 9 =====
Install small, weather-resistant labels near selected plants, subject to school approval. 
Each label could display the plant's common name, scientific name and a QR code linking to its 
profile on the biodiversity website. 
This would encourage students to recognize and appreciate the vegetation they encounter. 
Recommendation 3 — Establish Regular Plant Health Monitoring 
Create a student-led monitoring program that records changes in leaf condition, flowering, 
visible pests and environmental conditions. 
Monitoring should be carried out consistently so that observations can be compared over time. 
Potential concerns should be communicated to school maintenance staff rather than treated 
through unapproved interventions. 
Recommendation 4 — Encourage Ecologically Appropriate Planting 
Work with school staff to evaluate whether suitable native or locally beneficial plants could be 
introduced into appropriate planting areas. 
Species should be selected based on their ecological value, compatibility with the local 
environment, maintenance requirements and available space. 
This could help support local biodiversity while preserving the functionality of school facilities. 
Recommendation 5 — Organize Environmental Awareness Activities 
Conduct short educational campaigns through school announcements, posters, digital media or 
student presentations. 
Activities could encourage students to recognize plant species, respect landscaped areas and 
participate in non-invasive biodiversity observations.

Through identifying plant species and investigating their ecological significance, students can 
develop a stronger understanding of the importance of healthy ecosystems.

10. Service as Action Reflection 
This project encourages us to recognize that environmental responsibility extends beyond 
large-scale issues such as climate change and deforestation. It also involves appreciating and 
protecting the living organisms within our immediate surroundings. 
By examining the vegetation at Tunas Muda School, we developed a clearer understanding of 
how plants contribute to the environment and how scientific observation can reveal opportunities 
for improvement. 

===== PAGE 11 =====
Our research also highlighted the importance of distinguishing evidence from assumptions. 
Although photographs can reveal visible plant characteristics, they cannot independently explain 
the causes of plant damage or establish the overall health of an ecosystem. 
As a group, we aim to apply our research through environmental education, collaborative 
planning and realistic recommendations to school leadership. 
Our intended contribution is not simply to produce a report, but to encourage a lasting sense of 
responsibility toward the school's natural environment. 
11. Conclusion 
The photographic observations reveal a range of trees, palms and ornamental plants across 
Tunas Muda School's outdoor environment. 
These plants contribute to the school's vegetation structure, provide shade and offer potential 
ecological resources for other organisms. 
Although some plants display visible leaf discoloration or damage, the available evidence is 
insufficient to determine whether these observations indicate significant environmental 
problems. 
Our findings therefore emphasize the need for continued investigation, verified plant 
identification and regular environmental monitoring. 
By developing a biodiversity website, promoting environmental awareness and proposing 
practical conservation measures, our group hopes to encourage students to recognize the 
ecological importance of their surroundings. 
Ultimately, a more sustainable school environment depends not only on the presence of plants, 
but also on the knowledge, cooperation and responsibility of the community that shares the 
space.
